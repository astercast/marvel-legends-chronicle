New collector orientation

Start here. Buy smart.

Twenty-four years, 1,904 figures, and a whole vocabulary of variants, chases, and Build-A-Figures. This is the ten-minute orientation that keeps you from learning it all the expensive way.

Your first five pickups

Not a shopping list — five kinds of buys that teach you the hobby's mechanics. Every example below is a real release in this archive.

1

A current mainline favorite

Pick your favorite character's most recent mainline release — e.g. the 2024 Deadpool & Wolverine Wave 1 Wolverine. Current waves are the cheapest, easiest figures to find, and they teach you what modern articulation feels like.

2

A full BAF wave

Buy every figure in one Build-A-Figure wave — e.g. the 2025 Nemesis Wave (7 figures, builds Nemesis). Assembling the BAF is the line's signature ritual, and buying the wave together is almost always cheaper than hunting pieces later.

3

A retro-card release

Hasbro's retro cards (2024–2026) reissue beloved sculpts on vintage-style packaging. They're affordable, gorgeous on a shelf, and a low-stakes way to learn what "good paint" looks like. See the packaging guide for the era breakdown.

4

One Toy Biz classic, secondhand

A 2002–2006 Toy Biz figure — the line's roots. Buy loose, check the joints in person or ask for photos: 20-year-old plastic can be brittle. The timeline's era bands show you exactly which years are Toy Biz.

5

Something you just love

The hobby runs on joy, not portfolio theory. One figure bought purely because it makes you grin will outlast every "investment" on your shelf. Check it's not a bootleg first — step two, below.

How to read a wave

Every entry in this archive follows the same anatomy. Learn it once and you can read any listing:

  • Series + year — the wave's name and release year. Waves are the collecting unit; figures are rarely sold solo at retail.
  • Manufacturer — Toy Biz (2002–2006) or Hasbro (2007–today). Different eras, different joints, different packaging.
  • BAF piece — which Build-A-Figure part (if any) comes in the box. No piece, no completion.
  • Variant — a deco or accessory difference from the standard release in the same wave. Variants ship in the same case, usually one per case.
  • Exclusive — sold only through one retailer or convention. Harder to find later, and the first thing bootleggers copy.

Don't get burned: spotting fakes

Bootlegs target popular characters and exclusives. Before you buy secondhand, run the authentication wizard — it walks you through packaging tells, paint quality, joint feel, and the price-too-good test. The two rules that catch 90% of fakes:

  • Compare against a known-good photo — paint slop around the eyes and wrong plastic sheen are the classic tells.
  • Check sold listings, not asking prices — if the "deal" is half of what figures actually sold for, it's not a deal.

BAF math, before you spend

A Build-A-Figure only exists if you own every piece. Before committing to a BAF:

  • Count the pieces and which waves they're in — the BAF planner maps all 101 BAFs and 614 pieces.
  • Price the pieces you're missing, not the BAF. A "cheap" BAF with two rare pieces is an expensive BAF.
  • Check the Your Shelf "Almost there" cards — it tells you which BAFs you're already 1–2 pieces from finishing.

  • Compare — side-by-side figure comparisons for the hard choices.

Learn real prices, not asking prices

The single most expensive beginner mistake is paying an asking price. The fix is mechanical:

  • Read the price guide — it explains the sold-listing method and what the numbers actually mean.
  • Use the price journal — log sold listings you find, and it values your collection from your own entries. Every dollar traces to a real sale.
  • Rule of thumb: a figure is worth what the last 10–15 sold listings say, not what the current listings ask.
