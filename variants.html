Variant & Chase Identifier

Every documented variant and chase in the archive — what the variant looks like versus the regular release, and how to confirm one with the package in your hands.

How to spot a variant in the wild

1. Read the deco, not the name

Most modern variants are deco variants — same sculpt, different paint. Compare against the regular release: Stealth Iron Man is all-black where the regular is red-and-gold; the "No 4 on chest" Human Torch is missing its chest emblem. If the sculpt differs (different head, different body parts), it's a rarer sculpt variant — e.g., Weapon X Wolverine vs. Damaged Weapon X.

2. Check the package codes

Hasbro prints 5-digit SKU codes on figures (G/F prefix in recent years; older were all-numeric) that match the product number on the cardback. A variant has its own SKU. For Target exclusives, collectors use Target DCPIs (internal item numbers) to locate stock. Mismatched or missing codes are a red flag — see the Auth Wizard.

3. Know the case-ratio rule

A figure is a "chase" if it ships 1× per case while the rest run 1–2×, or only in revision assortments. Modern standard cases hold 8 figures (retailers publish the mixes). Hasbro publishes no official chase ratios — every ratio is reverse-engineered from case mixes, so treat any stated ratio as collector analysis, not fact.

4. Watch for revision cases

One wave can span multiple assortments, and revision cases swap variants in or out mid-wave. The classic: the 2007 Brood Queen wave's revision case added the Marvel Girl variant and Hydra Soldier variant at 1× each. A "wave 1" box and a "wave 1 revision" box can hold different figures.

5. Label and packaging variants

Sometimes the figure is identical and the package differs: Stealth Iron Man exists with and without "Stealth" on the package label; international releases swapped posters for comics; modern Retro cards rehouse existing sculpts. These matter mainly to carded collectors — loose buyers can ignore them.

6. What holds value

Collector guidance: deco variants — buy the look you like, premiums fade on reissue. Sculpt variants and true short-packs (1× per case or revision-only) hold value better. Unreleased prototypes: verify provenance before believing any price. Full taxonomy in the encyclopedia.
