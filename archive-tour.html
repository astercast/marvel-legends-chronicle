How the picks work — and why the chapters break where they do

The chapter breaks are set from the catalog's own wave data. 2002–2006 is the Toy Biz run: every wave in it is Toy Biz and every 2007 wave is Hasbro, so the manufacturer field draws the line — and the Build-A-Figure is born inside it, in 2005. 2007–2012 is Hasbro's lean early stretch, bottoming out in 2009–2011 and closing on the wave the catalog itself names the Return of Marvel Legends. 2013–2019 is the rebuild: the Build-A-Figure becomes the default architecture of a wave (46 of its 54 waves carry one) and annual output climbs to a 157-figure peak in 2018. 2020–2026 is the modern era — the largest chapter, with exclusives outnumbering the three earlier chapters combined. The four chapters partition the archive: every wave and every figure belongs to exactly one chapter.

Curator picks are selected by rule, the same six rules in every chapter, applied in this order and de-duplicated by figure:

  1. Opens the chapter — the first figure of the chapter's earliest wave.
  2. Closes the chapter — the first figure of the chapter's latest wave.
  3. Completes the first BAF — the last piece-carrying figure of the chapter's earliest Build-A-Figure wave: collect that wave in catalog order and this piece finishes the build.
  4. Completes the last BAF — the same rule on the chapter's latest Build-A-Figure wave.
  5. First variant — the first figure in the chapter carrying a recorded variant.
  6. First exclusive — the first figure in the chapter carrying a recorded exclusive.

A rule that finds nothing produces no pick, and the chapter says so on its face — the modern era records no variants, so its variant slot stands empty rather than filled by hand. Every count on this page is counted from the catalog itself, and there are no prices anywhere on the tour: this is the record, not the market.
