Methodology & honest gaps

How this page was built

  • Waves and figures come from data/figures.json — 5 waves carry a 2026 year, plus the Store & Convention Exclusives bucket's 2026 entries. Release dates, windows, and announced MSRPs come from data/preorders.json (21 tracked items); announced prices are never market prices.
  • Price movements use data/price-signals.json only — reported sold bands, averages, listings, and claimed prices with named sources. The Chronicle publishes no price index, and no 2026 figure has reported aftermarket data in the archive yet.
  • The Top 10 is scored, not vibes: each entry's documented distinctions are counted (first-ever figure = 2 points; first-since-a-year, notable pack-in, format distinction, archive-first, or documented 2026 market story = 1 each). Ties break alphabetically. Every distinction cites its record.
  • Best-value ranks announced per-figure MSRP only, using pack sizes stated in each item's own tracker record. No aftermarket data, no quality scores, no speculation.
  • KO coverage is the documented record: no new 2026 counterfeit wave was documented; specific KO listings are named with their sources. Rarity highlights come from the Rarity Guide's tiers.

What the data doesn't cover

  • Price data covers 7 figures — none of them 2026 releases. The Fall 2026 waves' aftermarket behavior will be the first real test of the reissue-cooling pattern at scale.
  • The encyclopedia's 2026 archive is 5 dated waves (114 figures). The pre-order tracker covers 21 items, 17 with 2026 release windows or dates; undated 2027 reveals are listed in the timeline, not ranked.
  • No production numbers or chase ratios were ever officially documented for any figure on this page — the Rarity Guide's methodology applies here too.
  • Observed October 11, 2026. The year isn't over: Q4 pre-order dates and December ship windows may still move.

Biggest surprises

  1. The VHS premiums collapsed by design

    The sold-out Pulse VHS-exclusive Animated Series figures escalated on the aftermarket — then Hasbro re-released the same figures on retro blister cards at $27.99. Sellout → spike → reissue → premium collapse, textbook and on schedule. Marvel Toy News — TAS reissues (Sep 2026)

  2. A white Doom sold for $200

    The white-deco Epic Heroes Doctor Doom — the modern chase-adjacent standout — closed at $200 on August 10, 2026, the top of its $115–$200 reported band. The standard green Doom averaged $62.44 across 48 tracked sales; the premium is the variant, not the character. actionfigure411 — Epic Heroes Dr. Doom white variant sold history

  3. Target cancelled confirmed preorders

    X-Men Onslaught was 'extremely hit or miss in stores' — and Target cancelled many confirmed 2026 preorders, so in-hand beats preordered. Availability, not price, was the 2026 Target story. Pre-order tracker: onslaught-target

  4. The year's biggest figure is made-to-order

    Bastion & Bolivar Trask: an 11.3-inch-winged Bastion, 10 accessories, $74.99, Hasbro Pulse only — and the order window closes November 20, 2026. Miss it and it's gone from retail; it ships November 2027. Pre-order tracker: bastion-trask-2pack

  5. The year of firsts

    First-ever Cypher. First-ever solo-carded 6-inch Aunt May. First red Elektra since 2018. First-ever Bastion (per the tracker research). 2026 kept handing collectors figures the line had never done. Pre-order tracker records

  6. Marvel Rivals joins the line

    Moon Knight, Punisher, Spider-Man, Luna Snow, Venom, and Jeff the Land Shark — six Marvel Rivals (video game) versions, the first in the archive, all inside the 2026 exclusives. Encyclopedia wave: hasbro-2026-exclusives

  7. Retro Venom comes back at $34.99

    The 2021 Pulse Con cel-shaded TAS Venom — a convention exclusive — returns at Fan Channel retailers for $34.99, shipping December 2026. The reissue program isn't slowing down. Bleeding Cool — Retro Venom reissue (Aug 2026)

  8. The modern chase is dead

The 2026 narrative

The year the chase died

Hasbro has 'largely abandoned' short-packed chase figures in modern Marvel Legends, and no 2025–2026-era 6-inch chase was documented by the hobby press this year. The chase era — Toy Biz's finite redistributions, the art-deco variants produced 'with scarcity in mind' — is now history, and the Rarity Guide treats it as such. What replaced it isn't a new scarcity mechanic: it's the order window. The year's defining limited release, the Bastion & Bolivar Trask 2-pack, is made-to-order — scarcity measured in calendar days, not case ratios.

Market briefing — the chase era ends Rarity Guide

Retro is the packaging story

2026 brought no packaging overhaul. Instead it brought the retro blister card back as the default canvas — the X-Men '97 wave, the Spider-Man: The Animated Series wave, and the Retro Venom reissue all ride retro cardbacks — and the mini comic as the pack-in of the year, with Mini Comics Wave 3 putting a physical comic in every box. After the plastic-free reversal of April 2023, the line's packaging story is settled: nostalgia on the front, sustainability in the materials.

The reissue machine

2026 was the year the reissue program stopped being a rumor and became the market's central mechanism. The Pulse VHS-exclusive Animated Series figures sold out, escalated on the aftermarket — and came back on retro blister cards at the $27.99 standard. The 2021 Pulse Con cel-shaded Venom is back at Fan Channel for $34.99. NYCC added an Astonishing X-Men two-pack reissue. The pattern is now textbook: sellout → aftermarket spike → reissue → premium collapse. The open question is whether it keeps working at scale — the Fall 2026 waves' aftermarket behavior will be the first real test.

The aftermarket in miniature

Reported-price coverage for 2026 is thin — seven figures, none of them 2026 releases — but it tells a coherent story. The white-deco Epic Heroes Doctor Doom is the modern chase-adjacent standout: $115–$200 in reported solds, closing at $200 on August 10, 2026, against roughly $60 in October 2024. The standard green Doom averaged $62.44 across 48 tracked sales. The $7,500 asking price on the unproduced Toy Biz Series 15 blue-and-white Wasp chase prototype (~10 carded) is an ask, not a sale — a two-decade-old artifact, not a 2026 discovery. And one retailer's October snapshot said it plainly: the older Colossus-BAF Cyclops sat in stock at $14.99 while the X-Men '97 Cyclops sold out. Demand concentrates where the cameras are.
