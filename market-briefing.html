Aftermarket · 2026

2026 market briefing.

What the Marvel Legends market actually looked like this year — chase figures, grails, reissue pressure, and knockoffs — from index-sourced hobby reporting read October 11, 2026. Every number here is a reported listing or sold, never an index.

Methodology

How to read this page

No index was found. There is no systematic 2026 gainers/losers study — reported auction data is limited to individual listings, and general trade commentary on appreciation rests on a single low-authority source. So: prices below are reported listings and solds with named sources, not trend lines. An "asking price" is not a sale. Figures labeled single-source came from one source only. See the Price Guide for the honest way to value your own figures.

Chase figures

The modern chase is effectively dead

Hasbro has "largely abandoned" short-packed chase figures in modern Marvel Legends. No 2025–2026-era 6" chase figure was documented by the hobby press this year.

The modern chase-adjacent standout is the Epic Heroes (3.75") white Dr. Doom — the "Future Foundation" variant — with reported eBay solds of $115–$200 (May–Aug 2026) against roughly $60 in October 2024, per actionfigure411's sold-history page. Treat that as reported solds on one tracker, not a valuation.

Grail of the season

The $7,500 Wasp prototype

An unproduced Toy Biz Marvel Legends Series 15 blue-and-white Wasp chase prototype — one of only ~10 carded examples known since 2006 — was listed on eBay at $7,500, reported as the single most sought-after ML figure. That is an asking price, not a completed sale, and the prototype is not a 2026 discovery — it is an active listing for a two-decade-old artifact.

Reissue pressure

Hasbro's reissue program is cooling aftermarket premiums

The Pulse "VHS"-exclusive Spider-Man: The Animated Series (and X-Men) figures escalated on the aftermarket after their sellouts. Hasbro's 2026 answer: re-release those same figures on retro blister cards (the TAS wave — Aunt May, Mary Jane, Smythe, Carnage — pre-ordered September 29, 2026), directly softening the availability-based premiums the VHS versions built up. The pattern is textbook: sellout → aftermarket spike → reissue → premium collapse.

The same vulnerability framing lives in the encyclopedia hall of fame: resilient premiums rest on true rarity, vulnerable ones rest on availability and can collapse when Hasbro reissues.

Knockoffs

The KO market is evergreen, not a 2026 wave

No notable 2025–2026-specific counterfeit wave was documented. What exists is the steady AliExpress KO trade targeting current hits:

  • Maximum Series Spider-Man KO — reviewed in video, ~April 2026.
  • X-Men '97 Cyclops KO (Mustang Toyz, S.H. Figuarts-style) — reviewed September 2026.
  • Older Spider-Man KOs (Pizza Spidey, Gamerverse, AND, 2099) and Carnage KOs with known telltales: silver eyes on Carnage; neck-ball/elbow peg differences and joint gaps on Spider-Man.

Community consensus (Toyark forums): buyer beware — inconsistent paint and brittle joints (one reported Black Cat KO leg snap). If a listing looks wrong, run it through the authentication wizard and the bootleg-spotting guide.

Price baseline

What things cost at retail in 2026

2026 MSRP baseline: $27.99 standard single figures; deluxe $34.99–$46.99 (Iron Spider Aaron Davis $34.99, Professor Hulk $46.99); Marvel Legends × MTG Wave 2 $39.99 with a playable card; Miles Morales Electronic Expressive Mask roleplay $139.99; X-Men '97 Apocalypse 12" Pulse exclusive $119.99. Fall 2026 pre-order wave pricing (Doomsday, X-Men '97, Mini Comics) holds the $27.99 standard.

Deal note (single source): on September 21, 2026, eBay seller "Toy Line Collectibles" ran up to 17% off across Marvel Legends, Star Wars, WWE, and Transformers lines.
