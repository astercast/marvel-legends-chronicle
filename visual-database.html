The archive, pictured

The Visual Database.

The Chronicle was a typographic archive for fifty-five doors — every figure filed in type. This door opens the picture room: each figure with a verified product photograph hangs here as an embed from the retailer or manufacturer that shot it, with its places to buy as outbound links and the price the retailer listed when the picture was filed. Nothing is sold here, nothing is checked out here — a database, the way BrickLink is a database. And every figure without a verified picture is not hidden: it is filed by name in the queue below, and everywhere else in this archive it renders in the same plain type it always did.

The picture census could not be counted

The figure archive did not load, so there is nothing honest to hang. The census is counted from that archive and its picture manifest — without them, this page says so rather than guessing.

The pictured core

The typographic shelf

The figures of the pictured waves whose pictures have not verified yet. They are not gaps in the wall — this is the archive's standard treatment, the one all 1,939 figures carried before this door opened, and the one any figure returns to if its embed ever dies.

The fill-in queue

Counted in this browser from the catalog itself (1,939 figures) and the picture manifest (data/v1/figure-images.json). A figure is pictured when the manifest holds an entry for it — and an entry is filed only after its embed URL answers live (HTTP 200, an image content type) with its source recorded: the retailer or manufacturer product page the photograph comes from, and the outbound place to buy. The photography is Hasbro's own product photography, embedded from the retailer — referenced, never copied into this archive, and never AI-generated. Prices are the retailer's own listed prices on the day they were recorded, shown beside the outbound link where they are checked; they are not this archive's prices, and this archive sells nothing. If an embed ever stops answering, its plate removes itself and the figure renders in type — the typographic shelf above is that fallback, shown on purpose. The queue runs nearest-complete first, then most recent first, so the fill-in order is a fact of the record. For the per-figure record behind every picture, read The Spec Sheet.
