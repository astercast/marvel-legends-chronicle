Collector's Craft · Wave 17

Display & storage.

The practical craft of keeping a collection alive: beating the sun, choosing cases, taming dust and damp, quake-proofing shelves, and rotating the shelf so it stays a joy instead of a maintenance project. Companion to the condition & grading guide and the encyclopedia's packaging evolution.

UV protection — beat the sun first

Direct sunlight is the #1 killer of collections. UV fades paint and packaging irreversibly — a figure that sat in a sunny window for a summer comes out permanently bleached, and no restoration brings it back. Before you spend a dollar on cases or lighting, fix where the sun lands.

Keep figures out of direct sun

Shelves on walls the sun never hits directly. If a shelf catches afternoon sun, move the shelf or the figures — curtains and blinds are a partial fix, not a solution, since UV gets through both.

UV film and UV glass

UV-blocking window film on nearby windows is cheap and effective for the room, and UV-protective acrylic cases exist for high-value pieces. Both slow fading; neither makes sun exposure safe, so distance from windows still comes first.

Boxed pieces need shade too

Carded figures fade through the blister — card art, bubbles, and price stickers all bleach. Storage boxes go against interior walls, never in front of windows.

Archival cases vs detolfs

Two philosophies: seal each piece individually, or put a glass wall between the shelf and the room. Most collectors end up with both — individual cases for the keepers, a cabinet for the shelf.

Individual acrylic cases

UV-protective, dust-proof, and stackable — the standard for carded figures worth protecting and for loose centerpieces. More expensive per figure, so reserve them for your best pieces (the ones you'd showcase on the Showcase page). Cheap non-UV acrylic yellows over time; check what you're buying.

Glass display cabinets (the Detolf style)

Tall glass-front cabinets are the collector staple for a reason: they hold a whole team per shelf, keep dust off everything at once, and look like a museum case. Anchor them to the wall — they're top-heavy. Keep them away from windows, since glass concentrates the UV problem.

Open shelves

Fine for the rotation shelf and play pieces, brutal for long-term display: dust settles into every joint and paint crease. If it's open, it's a display you dust, not storage — see the dust section below.

Humidity and temperature basics

Plastic is tough; packaging and paint are not. The enemy is extremes and swings — hot/cold and damp/dry cycling does the damage, not any one normal room.

Stable room temperature

A lived-in room is fine. Attics (heat) and garages (heat, cold, damp) are the two worst rooms in any house for a collection — heat warps cards and softens joints, and cycling does it faster than steady warmth.

Keep it dry, keep it stable

Damp breeds mold on card stock and can pit metal parts; bone-dry heat dries out stickers and decal edges. A normal indoor room with stable conditions beats any extreme. If a room smells musty, your cards are already losing.

Boxed storage rules

Storage boxes live in climate-controlled rooms: off the floor (in case of water), away from exterior walls, sealed against dust but not airtight in a damp room. Label the outside — you'll thank yourself in a year.

Dust management

Prevention beats cleaning

Cases and cabinets are dust management, not luxury. Every figure behind glass is one you never have to clean — that's the real argument for a cabinet.

Cleaning open displays

Soft makeup brushes or clean paintbrushes for figures, microfiber for shelves. Canned air works but hold it back — close blasts can snap thin accessories, and the propellant can chill-spot paint. A barely damp cloth is the ceiling for cleaning products, same rule as the grading don'ts.

Schedule it

Open shelves need a monthly pass; cased displays need a quarterly wipe of the case exterior. Put it on a schedule or it silently becomes a decade of dust baked into joint seams.

Earthquake-proofing shelves

Gravity plus a wobble is how display shelves become floor salvage. Everything here is cheap and invisible — do it once and forget it.

Museum putty on every figure

Removable mounting putty (sold as museum putty or quake wax) under each figure's feet is the single highest-value protection in the hobby. It holds figures through bumps and minor quakes, peels off clean, and costs almost nothing. Every loose figure on an open shelf gets putty.

Anchor tall furniture

Tall glass cabinets get wall-anchored, period — a toppling cabinet is a safety hazard, not just a collection loss. Bookcases and floating shelves get brackets rated for the load.

Heavy pieces go low

Big BAFs, deluxe sets, and anything with die-cast or dense plastic live on the lowest shelves. Light figures up high, heavy figures down low — the same rule as any bookcase.

Rotating displays

Keep the shelf fresh

Rotate a theme in and out — a team month, a villain month, a wave-completion celebration. The collection feels new without spending anything, and the pieces coming off the shelf get a dust pass on the way into storage.

Rotation is preservation

Every figure on display is slowly fading, even under good lighting. Rotating limits each piece's cumulative light exposure — and gives you a reason to re-pose, re-discover, and photograph pieces you'd forgotten.

Store the bench cleanly

Figures waiting their turn go in labeled boxes, not a pile on a closet shelf. The rotation box is where condition is made or lost — loose figures rubbing together in a box arrive at their next rotation a grade lower.

Photographing the collection

Diffused light, plain background

A white poster board and a bright lamp behind a bedsheet beats any fancy setup. Direct flash and harsh lamps create hot spots on bubbles and wash out paint — diffuse everything.

Shoot the shelf, not just the figures

Straight-on shelf shots document the collection; slight angles show depth. Photograph boxed storage too — a labeled-box photo is how you find things later, and it's your insurance record if anything happens to the room.

Share the keepers

Your best-posed, best-cased centerpieces belong on the Showcase — photograph them once well and the photos do double duty.

Room by room

  • Living room — the public gallery

    The Detolf-style cabinet room: sun-safe wall, anchored cabinets, dust handled by glass. Keep the rotation shelf here so guests see something new.

  • Office / study — the working shelf

    Open shelf for the current rotation and desk figures — museum putty on everything, monthly dust pass. Keep carded grails out of the desk blast zone (coffee, sunlight from the desk window).

  • Bedroom — the personal shelf

    Fine for display, same sun rule applies. Avoid the top of dressers under windows; a shaded wall shelf with putty'd figures is the move.

  • Closets and storage rooms — the archive

    Interior-wall closet with labeled, off-the-floor boxes: the right answer for the bench and the boxed backlog. Attic and garage are never the answer — heat, damp, and temperature swings.

Standard collector knowledge, kept general: these are practices the hobby has settled on over decades, not product endorsements and not investment advice. Your climate, your quakes, and your square footage set the details.
