Prices

Sold listings, not active listings. Active listings show what sellers hope to get; sold/completed listings show what buyers actually paid.

  1. eBay → advanced search → SOLD listings, last 90 days.
  2. Filter to your exact condition tier: loose complete / boxed / graded.
  3. Take the median of the last 10–15 solds, drop outliers.
  4. Asking prices are irrelevant, only solds count.
  • Every price carries: amount, condition tier, source, date observed.
  • "Asking" vs "sold" is labeled every time, never mixed.
  • Claims that rest on a single source, or on a tabloid report, are marked as unverified.
  • No price presented as current without an observation date.

Rarity words

  • Chase figures — Short-packed figures hidden in the regular assortment.
  • Running changes — Mid-production fixes and tweaks, both versions shipped at retail.
  • Deco variants — Same sculpt, new paint, sometimes deliberately scarce.
  • Unmasked variants — The masked figure, minus the mask, a whole sub-economy.
  • Convention exclusives — SDCC and friends: one weekend, one booth, then the aftermarket.
  • Store exclusives — Walmart, Target, Walgreens, TRU, Amazon, wide, but gated.
  • Pulse & Fan Channel — Direct-to-collector: the specialist channel.
  • HasLab — Crowdfunded, funded-only production, the true limited run.
  • Made-to-order — Order-window scarcity: the calendar is the chase ratio.

Spotting fakes

Packaging first

  1. Blurry printing or off-color logos on the card/box
  2. Missing Hasbro or Marvel markings and legal text
  3. Flimsy cardstock or thin, cloudy blisters

Paint & sculpt

  1. Rough edges and sloppy or missing paint applications
  2. Misshapen parts or wrong accessory details
  3. Authentic Legends have crisp, consistent deco throughout

Joints are the fingerprint

  1. KOs differ at the neck ball peg, elbow/knee pegs and hand pegs
  2. Visible gaps at elbows and knees
  3. Example: KO Carnage has silver eyes vs. the authentic paint

Known KO targets

  1. Dragon Man, Ronan, and the Juggernaut BAF recast are known KO targets
  2. If a rare figure sells at a common price, be suspicious

Listing red flags

  1. Seller located in China/Hong Kong with $10–40 pricing on a $25+ figure
  2. Stock photos only, no brand named in the description
  3. "No box" offers on supposedly mint exclusives

Price logic

  1. If it is far below retail or secondary market for a popular wave or variant, investigate before buying

Grading, briefly

AFA / CGA

AFA (Action Figure Authority) now operates as a division of Collectible Grading Authority (CGA), Roswell, Georgia. On Sept 22, 2026 CGA opened a New York drop-off office (grading still performed in Georgia). Tom Derby (founder, ex-Cloud City Collectibles) is a Partner at CGA. CGA also runs VGA (video games) and MGA (media, newly launched); it claims 25+ years via AFA/VGA.

CAS (Collector Archive Services)

Independent grading/authentication (collectorarchive.com). Anti-"U"-grade stance; offers a "preservation grade" for damaged-but-rare items.

Spotting variants

  • Deco variants (repaints) are the dominant modern chase form. Example: the ML1 Iron Man mold reused as gold armor with horned face mask, Walmart-exclusive Stealth Iron Man, Silver Centurion, and War Machine, same sculpt, different deco. The ML1 Iron Man was sculpted by Dave Cortez.
  • Sculpt variants are rarer and more valuable, e.g. Weapon X Wolverine vs. Damaged Weapon X (Giant Man series, Toy Biz), distinct sculpts.
  • Label variants: Stealth Iron Man exists with and without "Stealth" on the package label.

Line history

  • 1988 — Isaac Perlmutter's Toy Biz, successor to Charan/Chantex, holds the Marvel master toy license from 1990.
  • 1996 — Marvel files for bankruptcy; Toy Biz merges with Marvel Entertainment Group to form Marvel Enterprises, Toy Biz becoming its toy division.
  • 2002 — Marvel Legends launches as a Spider-Man Classics spin-off: clamshell packaging plus a 32-page reprint comic. Wave 1: Iron Man, Captain America, Hulk, Toad, aimed at adult collectors with best-in-class sculpt and articulation.
  • 2005 — Series 9 (Galactus series) packs one piece of an 18" Galactus per figure, the Build-A-Figure is born. Followed by the Sentinel, Apocalypse, Giant-Man, Onslaught, Mojo and M.O.D.O.K.
  • 2005 — Marvel terminates the Toy Biz Worldwide license a year early, paying a $13–16M penalty.
  • 2006 — Marvel grants Hasbro a 5-year master toy license: $205M guaranteed royalties, $100M non-refundable advance. Retail sales begin Jan 1, 2007 with Ghost Rider and Spider-Man 3 lines.
  • 2007 — New packaging drops the comic pack-in; new molds drop Toy Biz's signature finger joints. BAF continues with Annihilus, Brood Queen and others.
  • 2010 — Announced at SDCC 2010 after fan demand and MCU momentum; all-new sculpts return January 2012.
  • 2014 — Retooled as Marvel Legends: Infinite Series at $19.99 MSRP, box packaging, bigger BAFs, more accessories.
  • 2016 — Photo-real face-printing for MCU likenesses; butterfly joints; pinless joints debut ~2020 and become standard.
  • 2020 — First ML HasLab: 26.3" Sentinel at $349.99, funded in ~24 hours.
  • 2021 — 32" Galactus at $399.99 draws 14,000 backers, the biggest ML ever.
  • 2023 — 24" Giant-Man at $199.99 finishes with 13,876 backers. Ghost Rider's Engine of Vengeance becomes the line's only failed HasLab.
  • 2024 — Standard MSRP reaches $24.99; deluxe $30–50; box sets $40–90; HasLab $200–400.
