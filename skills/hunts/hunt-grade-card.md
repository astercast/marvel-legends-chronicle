# Grade card condition

## Use when

Buying or selling carded figures.

## Inputs

- The carded figure, good light

## Procedure

1. Corners: sharp? Blunt one corner = a full grade down.
2. Edges: shelf wear along the bottom is the common flaw.
3. Bubble: clear? Yellowed? Cracked? Lifting?
4. Card: price stickers, bends, spine stress — photograph all of it.
5. Grade honestly: C10 is a myth; most 'mint' is C8.
6. Know the collector scale: C10 (mint — essentially theoretical) down to C1 (destroyed); most traded cards land C6–C8.5.
7. Third-party grading: AFA now operates inside CGA (Roswell, GA; NY drop-off office opened Sept 2026) — 0–100 scale in 5s; sub-grades card (C) / bubble (B) / figure (F); 'Y' = yellowed bubble. Historic costs only: ~$16 standard / $28 express (old), ~$45+ with shipping (2008), up to ~$90–100 all-in for large items (2012) — verify current pricing before sending anything.
8. CAS (Collector Archive Services) is the independent alternative — BBTS partnered with CAS in Nov 2025, selling CAS-graded items with UV-blocking acrylic and digital certificates.
9. The no-premium caveat: NO Legends-specific graded-vs-ungraded sale premium has been documented. Grade for authentication, preservation, and buyer confidence — never pay to grade a $30 figure expecting a markup.

## Worked example

C8.5 assessment: one soft corner, clear bubble — priced as C8, sold fast.

## Pitfalls

- Calling everything mint — buyers distrust perfect grades on imperfect cards.
- Paying modern grading fees on a common figure — the math doesn't work.
