# Understand the counterfeit market

## Use when

Market awareness, complements the authentication checklists.

## Inputs

- None, read before hunting grails or deals

## Procedure

1. Know the vectors: AliExpress is the primary direct-to-consumer KO source (listings like 'Maximum Series Spider-Man'); Shopee (SE Asia) lists KOs at near-real prices (₱2,491–₱2,677 for a 'Retro VENOM', observed Oct 2026), the danger zone is KOs priced LIKE genuine, i.e. fraud, distinct from knowing KO purchase.
2. Know the price bands: ~$10–16 KO vs $25–100+ genuine (reviewer anecdote). A $40 'SDCC exclusive' is not a deal, it's a tell.
3. Know the quality spectrum (anecdotal): 'mixed bag', brittle plastic (KO Black Cat leg snapped in hand); Chinese Deadpool KOs reportedly better. Never assume 'good enough'.
4. Know the tells: neck ball peg differences, elbow/hand pegs, gaps at elbow/knee joints; KO Carnage has silver eyes (stillfigurineout.com comparisons).
5. Know the demand drivers: custom fodder (cheap kitbash bodies), stand-ins for expensive grails, knowing buyers accepting lower quality. Not every KO sale is a scam, but every misrepresented one is.
6. This is not new: MWCToys documented bootleg action-figure lines back in 2000. The modern shift is direct-to-consumer shipping from China.

## Worked example

Shopee listing at 90% of genuine price with stock photos: fraud-zone KO, run the full authentication wizard before even messaging the seller.

## Pitfalls

- 'Too good to be true' is always true in the KO market.
- Assuming all KOs are junk, some are good enough to fool you, which is exactly the problem.
