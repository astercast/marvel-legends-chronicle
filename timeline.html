The Legends Timeline

Packaging eras

Five packaging eras in twenty-five years — the box tells the story of the line as much as the figures inside.

Clamshell + comic

2002–2006

Oversized clamshell blisters with a 32-page reprint comic pack-in, inherited from Toy Biz's Spider-Man Classics line. [Wikipedia]

Blister card

2007–2013

Hasbro's packaging drops the comic pack-in; new molds drop Toy Biz's signature finger joints, but the Build-A-Figure theme continues. [Wikipedia]

Window box

2014–2022

The 2014 Infinite Series retool moves the line to box packaging with bigger BAFs and more accessories at a $19.99 MSRP. [Chronicle encyclopedia]

Plastic-free window box

2023–2025

Plastic-free packaging reversal: bioPET windows replace the open-window boxes. [Chronicle encyclopedia]

Retro blister + mini comic

2026–

2026 goes retro: blister cards with mini-comic pack-ins — a deliberate throwback to the Toy Biz clamshell era. [Chronicle encyclopedia]

2002 The line launches

Toy Biz

Marvel Legends debuts as a Toy Biz spin-off of the Spider-Man Classics line, aimed at adult collectors with best-in-class sculpt and articulation. Series 1 through 3 plus Walmart exclusives ship in oversized clamshells, each with a 32-page reprint comic book.

  • The line begins in 2002 as a spin-off of Spider-Man Classics, copying its clamshell packaging and included comic book. [Wikipedia — Marvel Legends Wikipedia — Spider-Man Classics]
  • The first wave ships: Iron Man, Captain America, and Hulk, with Toad as the villain. [Wikipedia — Marvel Legends]

Packaging: Oversized clamshell with 32-page reprint comic pack-in.

2005 Build-A-Figure is born

Toy Biz

Series 9 (the Galactus series) packs one piece of a 16-inch Galactus with every figure — the Build-A-Figure is born. The Sentinel and Apocalypse series follow the same playbook, and the Legendary Riders and Fearsome Foes box sets round out the year. Behind the scenes, Marvel terminates the Toy Biz Worldwide license a year early, paying a $13–16M penalty.

  • Series 9 introduces the Build-A-Figure: each figure carries a piece of a larger figure, so buying the full assortment assembles a character unavailable on its own — here, a 16-inch Galactus. [FigureRealm — Series 9 checklist Legendsverse — Galactus wave (2005) FigureList — Galactus BAF]
  • Marvel terminates the Toy Biz Worldwide license a year early, paying a $13–16M penalty. [Chronicle encyclopedia]

2006 Toy Biz's last stand

Toy Biz

The final Toy Biz year is a blaze of glory: Series 13 (Onslaught), 14 (Mojo), and 15 (M.O.D.O.K.), a Walmart-exclusive Giant-Man, and House of M, Monsters, and Young Avengers box sets. The Face-Off 2-pack program pairs arch-enemies, and the 12-inch Legends Icons line launches with Captain America, Iron Man, Wolverine, Hulk, Venom, Beast, and Spider-Man. Marvel grants Hasbro a 5-year master toy license — $205M in guaranteed royalties with a $100M non-refundable advance — with retail sales to begin January 1, 2007.

  • Face-Off 2-packs debut: Series 1 pairs Hulk vs. Leader, Captain America vs. Red Skull, and Daredevil vs. Kingpin; Series 2 adds Punisher vs. Jigsaw, Wolverine vs. Sabretooth, and the Iron Man/War Machine vs. Mandarin sets. [Legendsverse — Face-Off Captain America & Red Skull (2006) FigureRealm — Face-Off Cap vs Red Skull]
  • Marvel Legends Icons launches: 12-inch figures with over 30 points of articulation and an 'Evolution of an Icon' comic-sized book per character. [Wikipedia — Marvel Legends ToyWiz — Icons Hulk (Dec 2006)]
  • Marvel grants Hasbro a 5-year master toy license: $205M guaranteed royalties, $100M non-refundable advance. Retail sales begin January 1, 2007. [Chronicle encyclopedia]

2007 Hasbro takes over

Hasbro

The Hasbro era begins January 1, 2007. New packaging drops the comic book pack-in and new molds eliminate Toy Biz's signature finger joints — but the Build-A-Figure theme continues with the Annihilus, Blob, Ronan, Brood Queen, and Sandman (Spider-Man 3) waves, plus the first SDCC and store exclusives.

  • Hasbro becomes the license holder on January 1, 2007. The new packaging drops the comic book, new molds eliminate finger joints — a mainstay of the Toy Biz era — and the company continues the Build-A-Figure theme. [Wikipedia — Marvel Legends]

Packaging: Blister card; comic pack-in dropped; finger joints eliminated on new molds.

2020 HasLab era begins

Hasbro

The first Marvel Legends HasLab: a 26.3-inch Sentinel at $349.99, funded in about 24 hours. Mainline waves include Super-Skrull, Demogoblin, Crimson Dynamo, the Gamerverse Abomination, Venompool, and Joe Fixit waves, plus Sugar Man and Strong Guy. Hasbro also launches the 3.75-inch Marvel Legends RETRO line via Pulse two-packs, and at Toy Fair 2020 the X-Men movie likenesses finally join the 6-inch line.

  • First ML HasLab: 26.3-inch Sentinel at $349.99, funded in ~24 hours. [Chronicle encyclopedia]
  • Hasbro launches Marvel Legends RETRO, a 3.75-inch Kenner-style sister line — first as Pulse-exclusive two-packs (Cyclops, Captain America, Electro), then as a full line in 2021 starting as a timed Target exclusive. [Marvel Toys Wiki — Marvel Legends RETRO]
  • At Toy Fair 2020, Hasbro reveals X-Men movie likenesses for the line — Wolverine, Mystique, Professor X, Magneto (with dual-era head sculpts), plus the Deadpool 2 cast. [ComicBook.com — Toy Fair 2020]
